# Literature audit for deletion protocols under context compression

The current paper asks how closely two compressed-context deletion protocols reproduce full-context singleton deletion effects. Both protocols start with exactly the same selected evidence and score the same fixed answer tokens. Frozen deletion removes the selected chunks from a source. Recomputed deletion removes the source from the candidate pool, refits the ranker and packs the remaining evidence. The full-context reference deletes the source from the supplied passages. All three effects concern the tested reader and answer target.

## Claim and interpretation

Let S(C) denote the factual compression of source pool C, D_i delete source i from the pool, and F_i remove i's selected tokens from S(C). Both compressed effects subtract a changed-context score from the same factual score. The signed difference between recomputed and frozen effects is therefore score(F_i(S(C))) minus score(S(D_i(C))). This identity exposes where the policies differ. Its value on a question can move recomputed effects toward or away from the raw reference. An omitted source gives exactly zero under frozen deletion and may still shift selection under recomputation. This motivates reporting error and rank recovery rather than interpreting every nonzero recomputation effect as spurious.

The defensible empirical contribution is the relative fidelity of these two protocols under compression for an explicitly defined raw-context audit. The stronger scope requires replication across selectors and benchmarks. A system-removal question in which the selector would run again can legitimately call for the recomputed effect. The full-context reference defines the audit target here and carries no claim of ground-truth source ownership or citation correctness.

The frozen Hotpot protocol tests the primary MAE difference in 400 held-out questions for each reader. If both readers satisfy the locked criterion, lead with the Hotpot replication and retain the previously recorded MuSiQue limitations. If either misses, report reader-specific estimates and narrow the claim to the observed contexts. The models share questions. Preserve the zero-effect baseline and the distinction between factual QA and audit fidelity in either outcome.

The most informative extension after the locked review is one trained sentence selector with auditable chunk ownership, using both deletion protocols on its identical factual selection. Add joint deletion as a secondary study when discussing redundant or interacting evidence. Keep the replacement and positional findings as exploratory ablations given the donor uncertainty and numerical validation failures. The literature search establishes these overlaps and gaps in the papers checked, rather than priority over all prior work.
