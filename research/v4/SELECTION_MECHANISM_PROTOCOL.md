# Separate fixed-ranking repacking from refitted selection

Adaptive follow-up specified after all four FP32 frontier outcomes, before this follow-up runs. Larger 128-to-1024 budgets improve Hotpot but do not reliably improve MuSiQue. Frozen 512 improves the point estimate on all four studies, with exploratory intervals excluding zero on Llama/Hotpot and both MuSiQue studies; Qwen/Hotpot remains uncertain. These are different intervention contracts, not a proven uniquely correct attribution method. All MuSiQue methods remain worse than the zero-effect NMAE reference. The narrower question is which selector behavior produces the measured difference.

One new branch: keep the **original factual sentence ranking and its context-fitted IDF fixed**, remove candidates from the deleted source, then rerun the same greedy token-budget packing on the surviving ranking at cap 512. Call this fixed-rank repacking. Its factual token stream must exactly match the archived 512 frozen/recomputed stream and hash. It does not refit IDF or change sentence priority, but it may choose different replacement chunks and lengths after deletion. Greedy packing can displace other selected chunks, so this is a repacking control rather than a claim that only newly added chunks differ.

Compare three contracts on the same factual memory and fixed FP32 raw answer target: frozen deletion of selected chunks; fixed-rank repacking; refitted ranking/repacking. These let us contrast removing selection adaptation, retaining packing adaptation only, and retaining both ranking and packing adaptation. They do not isolate token positions, semantic content and post-deletion lengths, and algebraic effect differences are not unique causal mediation. Identical factual QA is true by construction and does not establish a deployable accuracy improvement.
